01Personal

I was a nerd growing up (and still am): straight-A student, glued to video games, falling asleep to math and science videos. I took apart laptops and jailbroke iPhones just to see what was possible, so studying electrical engineering made sense.

Music started with the viola in 5th grade. In middle school I tried French Horn, which I was terrible at, then percussion, which clicked immediately. Drumline took over my life, from scholastic circuits to independent organizations in college, with countless hours alone in practice rooms. Then COVID hit, the music world paused, and I put down my sticks.

A few years later I was out of college, working a corporate job I hated, and going a little crazy without connecting the dots. I bought a guitar and some pedals, then a drum kit, then a bass, and before long I was back in school. Getting back to playing brought me back to myself. Now I play in a few bands, I'm building toward a career in production and audio engineering, and I've put together a small studio in my apartment.

These days I work as a technical writer until AI takes my job, live in north central Austin, and I'm in school for music performance and audio engineering. I've seen 400+ artists live and I'm a Spurs fan, GSG.

I don't find myself all that interesting, but I'm always happy to talk if anyone's curious.

03Engineering

I studied Electrical Engineering at UTSA and graduated in 2022 with concentrations in Electronic Materials and Devices and in Digital Signal Processing. Coursework ran from circuit analysis and control theory to nanomaterials, semiconductor physics, and analog circuit design. I always gravitated toward analog over digital because it was easier for me to visualize, though I still worked through digital courses like VLSI.

During the school year I also worked in Dr. Ahn's Nanoelectronics Lab at UTSA, where I sat in on graduate courses in quantum physics and spintronics.
